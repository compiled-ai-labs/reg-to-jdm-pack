# What I do with this

Boris Teplitsky, independent cloud architect, boristep@gmail.com

## Compiling your text

Regulations, agency guides, investor guidelines, internal policy. The output is a pack like this one: rules in your engine's format, a receipt per rule, a question per unclear sentence with its readings, test cases, a readback for sign-off, and a list of the rules added, changed and retired when the source changes.

## Other rule engines

The pack format is engine-neutral; the emitter, the expression gate and the test runner are written per engine. This has been done for GoRules JDM. It can be done for any engine with a documented rule format and expression language: decision-table products, agent platforms with a rules node, mortgage QC and eligibility engines, in-house engines.

## Your vocabulary

Defining the fields, derived values and functions your rules may use, from your data model, so that a rule that compiles is a rule that runs.

## Answering the questions

Working with your analysts on the readings, or doing a first pass from domain knowledge in lending and banking regulation, with every decision recorded and signed.

## Updates

When the source changes, the pack is compiled again against the previous one. Earlier decisions carry forward, and the change list shows which rules were added, changed or retired.

## What I do not do

No model in the decision path. No runtime of my own; your engine runs the rules. No borrower or customer data reaches the compile.

## How to start

Send one document and the field list of your engine. I reply within a few days with what a pack for it would contain, the open questions I expect, and a fixed scope.
